The Problem

AI is changing everything.
Not everyone has a path through it.

01

AI is rewriting what work looks like — but not everyone is being brought along

AI is reshaping how work gets done across every sector, creating real opportunities for organizations and individuals who can use it well. But access to that opportunity isn't evenly distributed. For young people entering the workforce, the question isn't whether AI matters — it's whether they'll have the experience to show for it when it counts.

02

Those most affected have the least support

The people whose jobs and futures are most disrupted by AI — young people, first-generation students, communities without access to tech networks — are also the least likely to get hands-on AI experience before they enter the workforce. A class about AI is not the same as working with it on a real problem.

03

Employers know exactly what they want — and can't find it

70% of employers now use skills-based hiring. What moves a hiring manager isn't a certificate — it's documented work on a real problem, with a real client, and the judgment to know when AI helps and when it doesn't. Nearly 90% of recruiters want evidence of problem-solving. Only 55% think recent graduates can demonstrate it.

Capstone.AI is built on a simple idea: bring those young people and those organizations together. Students get the real-world AI experience employers are asking for. Nonprofits get the implementation support they can't otherwise access. Everyone moves forward.

What we deliver
Service 01

Outreach & Community Engagement

Reaching new audiences is the most common challenge non-profits report. A two-person team learns your mission, builds an AI-assisted content and outreach system tuned to your voice, and hands off everything so it keeps running after the semester ends. An expert reviews all deliverables before handoff.

  1. We learn your mission, your audience, and what's worked before
  2. We build and deploy a content and outreach system using standard AI tools
  3. We hand off everything — templates, prompts, workflows — so you can run it yourself

Throughout the engagement, students practice client communication and stakeholder management — not just at delivery, but at every check-in along the way.

Service 02

Data Analysis & Impact Reporting

Most non-profits collect more data than they use. A two-person team audits what you already have, identifies the 2–3 questions it should be answering, and builds a clean dashboard you can use for internal decisions and external reporting. An expert validates the analysis before delivery.

  1. We identify the 2–3 questions your data should be answering but isn't
  2. We build a clean analysis using standard tools (Google Looker Studio, Sheets, or equivalent)
  3. We document how to read it and update it — so it stays useful after delivery

Translating data findings for a non-technical client is a core part of the project. Students learn to present, defend, and adjust their analysis in real conversations — not just hand off a dashboard.

Service 03

Content & Communications at Scale

Most non-profits don't have the time or budget to produce content consistently. A two-person team extracts your voice, builds a content system around it, and hands off templates, prompts, and workflows so you can produce without starting from scratch every time.

  1. We extract your voice, tone, and what makes your organization distinct
  2. We build a content system — templates, formats, and an AI workflow tailored to your channels
  3. We deliver a complete handoff kit so content production doesn't stop when we do

Students learn to represent an organization's voice responsibly — including knowing when to override AI-generated copy and when the human judgment call matters most.

For Tech Companies

The tools are reaching nonprofits.
The implementation isn't.

Google, Microsoft, and Salesforce have invested hundreds of millions in getting AI tools into the hands of nonprofits. The access problem is being solved. What remains unsolved — and what no grant or software credit can fix — is the human implementation gap: someone embedded in a specific organization, learning its context, and actually making the technology work.

What tech companies are doing

Massive investment in nonprofit AI access

Google.org has deployed $75M through its AI Opportunity Fund and a $30M Generative AI Accelerator. Microsoft partnered with 375,000 nonprofits globally, providing $4.7B in donated and discounted technology. Salesforce committed $17M to 18 nonprofits for AI readiness. The tools, credits, and training are reaching the sector.

What isn't working yet

Access doesn't close the implementation gap

92% of nonprofits now use AI tools in some capacity. Just 7% report major improvements in organizational capability. 85% are exploring AI tools — only 24% have a formal strategy. The gap isn't access to technology. It's the absence of someone who can translate that technology into the specific workflows, voice, and constraints of each organization.

Where Capstone fits

The implementation layer tech companies can't provide at scale

Tech company initiatives are built for breadth — grants, accelerators, and credits that reach thousands of organizations. Capstone provides depth: a dedicated team that spends a full semester inside one organization, building something that works for them specifically. We're not a replacement for tech company programs. We're what makes them land.

Partnership opportunity

Sponsor the implementation layer

Tech companies with nonprofit AI initiatives can partner with Capstone to extend the impact of their programs — funding engagements, co-designing toolsets, or connecting their nonprofit grantees with Capstone student teams. Your tools. Our implementation. Documented outcomes both sides can point to.
